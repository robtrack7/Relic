## Desk & Boards

The **Desk** is the default infinite plane — what the GM is currently thinking about. A **Board** is a remembered composition: *the docks*, *the succession crisis*, *everything connected to the bells*. A Board contains nothing; it remembers which parts of the world were visible, where they sat, and how they related to the problem being explored. Delete the Board and every object survives.

Although technically infinite, the canvas should behave emotionally like a **private working desk**: loose composition, small piles, clusters, trays, stacks, surrounding annotations, temporary working groups. Do not render a wooden desk — the metaphor lives in behaviour and composition.

Support: buttery pan, cursor-anchored zoom, zoom-to-fit, zoom-to-selection, home, lasso and multi-select, alignment, distribution, grouping, drag-duplication where appropriate, visual connection, annotation, keyboard nudging, command palette, undo.

**Constellation behaviour.** Relationships render as delicate connections with variable cluster density, enormous negative space and selective labels. Never render every known relationship at once. A selected object's neighbourhood softly emerges around it; far zoom may reveal world structure as constellations; near zoom returns attention to discrete objects. The graph is a revealed property of the world, not the wallpaper.

**Target composition at rest:** a large warm sheet of intelligent space holding a number of beautifully composed artifacts, with the suggestion that a much larger world extends past the viewport. Some regions hold arranged cards. Some hold only a photograph and a handwritten thought. Some are empty.

## Focus

Selection deepens rather than departs. A focused card expands spatially or opens a nearby contextual surface while the surrounding Board stays perceptible — use subtle depth, framing or local desaturation, never an anonymous dark overlay.

Focus can reveal richer prose, claims, relationships, history, provenance, sources, revisions, editing and Librarian actions.

**Pull the thread.** Focus is universal and composable: hold one object and everything irrelevant recedes, leaving its appearances, relationships, places, promises, sessions and fragments. Add a second object and the world contracts to what connects them. The purpose is to make a large world locally comprehensible.

**Lenses.** Time, geography and relationships are projections of the same world — a time lens reveals events and causality, a map lens reveals places and routes, a relationship lens reveals allegiance, debt and influence. Each uses its natural visual language. Identity and selection persist across a lens change: if an object was selected before, it stays selected after. Changing lenses should feel like turning the world in your hands.

## Prep

Prep is assembling tonight's working materials on one side of the Desk — spatial, but more constrained and purposeful than free worldbuilding. The GM gathers and orders scenes, characters, places, unresolved Threads, images, notes, reference material, sources, reminders and decisions.

Use shallow stacks, ordered groups and clear session hierarchy. The Librarian may pull relevant material in, always showing exactly what it added and why.

**Prep composes directly into Stage.** Objective, opening material, active Threads, pinned objects, imagery and reminders arrive on Stage as projections of the same material — not copies, not a parallel session database. Changes made before going live stay connected to their source objects. The handoff should feel ceremonial but lightweight: the desk has been arranged, and the GM opens the window into Stage.

## Stage

Stage is Relic at the table: a quiet, cinematic control surface for running the world in real time. The Desk is expansive and contemplative; Stage is compressed, performative and interruption-safe. It should feel like **the same instrument after the lights go down** — not a switch into a game HUD.

### Rules

1. Every interaction is a glance, not a workflow.
2. One continuous live surface. No nested navigation or tab mazes.
3. Progressive disclosure over density — smallest useful form first, deepen in place.
4. Capture before classification. Seconds to record something, no schema decision.
5. The world provides the drama. Imagery and typography may intensify; controls stay calm and modern.
6. Stage closes the continuity loop — what happens here becomes reviewable evidence.

### Visual character

Inverse of the Desk: `--charcoal` / `--carbon` resting field, warm paper and cream for text and objects. Large photographic or illustrated world imagery when contextually meaningful; broad negative space; selective editorial serif for scene-setting and narrative fragments; soft grain; dark framing; circular apertures or spatial crops that reveal a place, person or mood; deliberate scale shifts; slower transitions between meaningful states.

Imagery is **context, not wallpaper**. A place, active scene, person or artifact may temporarily become the visual field behind the working material — the UI sits beside or around it rather than carpeting it with overlays. Reuse the same typography, object identity, metadata language, tokens, icons and interaction principles as everywhere else.

Density runs **lower than the Desk at any single moment**. The same rich world remains reachable; only the current slice is visible.

### Live composition

- **Session frame** — title, current state, recording / sync / offline status.
- **Persistent search** — instant retrieval across the Saga and relevant world context.
- **Session material** — objective, opening scene, scene notes, active Threads, key reminders from Prep.
- **Pinned World Objects** — the characters, places, factions, artifacts and Threads needed tonight.
- **Session capture** — quick notes, improvised facts, quick stubs.
- **Persistent live actions** — Record, Mark Moment, Capture, basic dice.

On mobile this resolves into **one vertical reading surface with a small sticky action cluster**. Tablet and desktop may add a restrained side carriage for one focused object; the main Stage stays single-focus.

### Objects on Stage

The same identity at three resolutions:

- **Collapsed** — name, type, role, one live-state cue.
- **Expanded** — only what is useful at the table: summary, wants, knows, key relationships, reminders, GM notes.
- **Focused** — the full canonical object, only when deliberately asked for.

Denser in relevance, looser in visual density: fewer fields, larger type, larger targets, stronger hierarchy. Selection never ejects the GM to a management page, and collapsing back to the running table is always one action. Stage-local emphasis is temporary attention, not truth.

### Capture and improvisation

Play creates material faster than anyone can organize it. A quick capture may be a sentence, fragment, decision, name, promise or unresolved fact. A quick stub creates the smallest useful person, place, faction, artifact or Thread. Both stay **Pencil** until later. No entity forms during play.

Recording is quiet and unmistakable — easy to see, start and stop, never theatrically animated. **Mark Moment** creates a lightweight temporal anchor for Review with one gesture and no explanation. Live transcription must never become a visual dependency of Stage.

Spoken words are not automatically truth. The GM narrates, jokes, speculates and corrects themselves. Truth still needs a deliberate act — *Librarian, Orin is the locksmith, add him.* **Truth can be effortless. It cannot be accidental.**

### Search

The most important intelligent action during play. Near-instantaneous across names, prose, notes and remembered context, without the GM knowing where an object lives. Results open as Stage projections, never as navigation away from Stage.

### End Session

The seam where live play stops being ephemeral and becomes evidence. Deliberate, safely reversible. Afterwards Relic processes the recording, marks, captures, notes and Prep context, and the Librarian reconstructs what happened, which objects changed, which appeared, which Threads advanced or stalled, what promises and consequences are outstanding, and what will matter next time.

These are **proposals, not truth**.

## Review

Post-session review happens **in the world**, not primarily in an approval inbox.

Turn on **Since last session**: new things emerge, changed things carry fresh marks, Threads show their movement, proposed relationships appear faintly where they belong. The GM walks the world noticing what changed. Group changes by story, not by database field.

Direct GM assertions may already be Ink. Evidence and Librarian inference appear in Pencil. Correcting one source fact recomputes downstream Pencil inference; anything unresolved may stay Pencil indefinitely.

Keep a quiet completeness index — `14 changes · 10 reviewed · 4 unresolved`. The index tells the GM whether they are finished; the world tells them what happened.

## Narrow screens

Preserve **workflow parity, not canvas parity**. Do not squeeze an infinite canvas onto a phone and pretend it works. Translate Relic into ordered object stacks, Board outlines, search, capture, Focus, editing, Prep, history, Librarian and Review. The underlying objects are identical to desktop.

A Board becomes an ordered spatial reading of its contents. Focus may go full-screen. Inspectors become sheets. The Librarian becomes a collapsible sheet.

**Stage is the exception — mobile is its primary form.** Build it native to one-handed, low-attention table use: one principal vertical surface, large tap targets, persistent search, progressive-disclosure Stage cards, a small sticky live-action bar, immediate capture and quick stubs, recording state visible without dominating, robust offline and reconnect behaviour, and no dependency on canvas gestures. Avoid sheets over sheets: search, open, capture, record, mark and return — each one step, each reversible.
