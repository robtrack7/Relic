# Relic UI — Visual Reference

Load this only when deeper visual or interaction guidance is useful.

## Cards and objects

Think:

**index card × museum object plate × contemporary editorial layout**

Prefer strong typography, clean edges, moderate architectural radii, shallow stacking, deliberate image crops, minimal borders, and excellent whitespace.

Different objects may deserve different representations:

- image-heavy objects can become nearly photographic;
- narrative cards may emphasize prose;
- source cards may expose provenance;
- unresolved material may be deliberately sparse;
- compact cards may show only the smallest useful identity.

**Pencil** should feel provisional, not disabled: lighter hierarchy, interrupted/dashed detail, subtle patterning, or explicit Pencil metadata. AI proposals should appear in place as provisional material before establishment.

Do not turn every piece of information into a bordered card.

## Spatial behaviour

The Desk is technically infinite but should emotionally behave like a working tabletop.

Encourage loose clusters, piles, visual territories, stacks, annotations, selective connections, and source material.

Relationship lines are contextual, not wallpaper. Do not render the whole graph by default.

Semantic zoom may shift emphasis:

- far: territories, clusters, major Threads;
- normal: Cards and local relationships;
- close/Focus: prose, provenance, history, sources, controls.

Spatial memory should help retrieval but must never be required. Search can always recover an object.

## Librarian

The Librarian is intelligence woven through Relic.

It occupies a persistent, collapsible carriage. Current World, Board, Focus, and selection are implicit context.

Prefer action in place: move/reorganize Cards; create or edit Pencil; connect material; gather Prep; navigate or Focus; answer briefly when prose is genuinely the best medium.

AI changes stay visible, attributable, reversible, and interruptible.

Avoid a separate visual universe for AI: no avatar blobs, sparkles, glows, or magical chrome. Relic Orange may indicate restrained activity.

## Stage

Stage may be more cinematic than the Desk.

Use, when useful: large world imagery, dramatic crops, selective serif typography, subtle grain, slower transitions, and stronger atmosphere.

Stage should feel like **opening a window from the Desk into the fictional world**.

Do not import Stage's cinematic density back into everyday editing.

## Density

Default to moderate information density with progressive disclosure.

A complex world should not require a complex-looking interface. Add persistent controls only when repeated use proves they deserve permanent space.

## Early implementation rule

During V2 build-out, coherent experiments are better than premature design-system bureaucracy.

Create reusable primitives when patterns are actually recurring. Until then, optimize for a strong working interaction and visual coherence.
