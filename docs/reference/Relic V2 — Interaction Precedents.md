# Relic V2 — Interaction Precedents

Relic should feel like **a living fictional world spread across a private working desk**: spatial, fluid, direct, and quietly intelligent.

These precedents establish the interaction direction, not a feature checklist.

### Scrintal — the primary workspace precedent

Take the **content-rich card on an infinite visual board**: thoughts can begin loosely, cards can deepen into substantial material, connections are visible, and the same underlying card can appear in multiple compositions without duplication.

Relic's Desk and Boards should inherit this immediacy, but its cards represent persistent world identities rather than notes.

### Heptabase — one world, many arrangements

Take the idea of **one knowledge network viewed through many whiteboards**. An object remains itself wherever it appears; the board is a way of seeing and arranging it, not where it lives.

This is the clearest precedent for Relic's rule that **the canvas is a view, not the database**.

### Miro — canvas physics and AI agency

Take the quality of the **infinite canvas**: frictionless pan, zoom, selection, movement, grouping, and direct manipulation. Take also the emerging model of AI that understands canvas context and can act within the canvas rather than producing isolated chat output.

Do **not** inherit Miro's enterprise-whiteboard density, template/tool clutter, or generic-object feel.

### Allume — temperament

Take the low-chrome, spatial **“messy middle”**: nested spaces, cards, loose material, and the feeling that organization can emerge after thought rather than before it.

### Relic's synthesis

**Desk** — the default infinite spatial workspace.
**Boards** — saved compositions of the same world.
**Cards** — representations of fragments and world identities, not duplicated records.
**Focus** — deeper inspection without losing the surrounding world.
**Librarian** — a persistent, collapsible contextual operator that can read and act on the current selection and canvas.
**Library/Search** — an index into the world, not its primary home.
**Stage** — the same world narrowed for live play.

Spatial arrangement is freeform thinking. **It does not itself create canon.** Semantic relationships, claims, events, Ink, and Pencil remain explicit world operations.

The target is **not “Miro for tabletop RPGs.”**

It is **Scrintal-like spatial thought + Heptabase-like persistent identity + Miro-quality canvas interaction + Relic's own world model and Librarian intelligence**, expressed with considerably less chrome and a more atmospheric, authored character.
